About

About me

I am a PhD candidate in the Psychological and Brain Sciences department at Johns Hopkins University. I graduated from Cardiff University with an undergraduate degree in Psychology, before obtaining a Masters by Research at Durham University. I then joined Dr. Chris Krupenye’s Social & Cognitive Origins group at Johns Hopkins where my work focused on comparative social cognition with a particular focus on theory of mind and the representations that underpin human and nonhuman mindreading.

I am broadly interested in understanding the continuities and discontinuities in social cognition across humans and other primates. Human sociality is remarkable, and although the sophistication of human cognition may be unparalleled in many respects, we are far from the only animals to demonstrate complex social-cognitive abilities. A major focus of my work is theory of mind, the ability to represent and reason about the mental states of others. Possessing a theory of mind allows an individual to make inferences about what another wants, needs, intends, sees, thinks, knows and believes! In a dynamic social world, this is a vital skill that makes an unpredictable world a little more manageable!

Outside of research and academia, I love cooking, baking, fresh air, and watching just about any kind of movie!

Research

Research Interests

My current research interests include (but are not limited to): theory of mind abilities across species, (dis)continuities in representational capacity and access, cognitive architecture, altercentrism, metacognition, cooperation, and the use of implicit measures of cognition (incl. eye tracking and pupillometry).

Important to me

Values

Academia can be an unknown and isolating environment, so it is pivotal we all work toward for everyone. My key values in research and science surround trying to make science accessible to all.

Accessibility

Academia can be an unfamiliar and isolating environment, and I believe we all have a responsibility to make it more accessible and supportive. Providing meaningful opportunities at every stage is essential if people from all backgrounds are to have an equal chance to participate and succeed. Science progresses through the diversity of people, perspectives, and experiences that contribute to it, and one of my core values is helping to create research environments where more people can take part.

Considered Communication

Related to accessibility, science can only be accessible to all if it considers its audience. Gone should be the days that scientists write only for other scientists. In a field packed with complex concepts and terminologies, it can be intimidating to those unfamiliar or new to the field or academia as a whole. As someone with dyslexia, I know from experience how important it is to consider accessibility when science writing, and it is something I have tried to work on in my current work and I aspire to always improve for future work.

Mentoring

I have taken an active role of mentoring throughout my journey in postgraduate studies, working closely some of the most amazing and talented students I have met. My previous mentors are a huge reason I am the researcher and man I am today, and I benefited from the time and energy they were able to provide for me. As a result, I am passionate about passing this on with the next rising generation of students and young scientists.
